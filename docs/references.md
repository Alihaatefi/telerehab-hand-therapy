# References

Literature surveyed for the thesis's review chapter. The PDFs themselves are
copyrighted by their publishers and are **not** redistributed here; each entry
links to the publisher's page.

## Telerehabilitation systems and platforms

1. **Evidence-based position paper on Physical and Rehabilitation Medicine (PRM)
   professional practice on telerehabilitation: the European PRM position (UEMS
   PRM Section)**
   M. Zampolini, A. Oral, N. Barotsis, C. Aguiar Branco, H. Burger, P. Capodaglio,
   F. Dinçer, A. Giustini, X. Hu, I. Irgens, S. Negrini, P. Tederko, I. Treger,
   C. Kiekens.
   *European Journal of Physical and Rehabilitation Medicine*, 60(2), April 2024.
   — The position paper framing what telerehabilitation is expected to deliver
   clinically; used to justify the remote-monitoring requirement.

2. **Telerehabilitation: Development, Application, and Need for Increased Usage
   in the COVID-19 Era for Patients with Spinal Pathology**
   B. Fiani, I. Siddiqi, S. C. Lee, L. Dhillon.
   *Cureus*, 12(9):e10563, September 2020. Open access (CC-BY 4.0).
   [doi:10.7759/cureus.10563](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.10563)
   — Scoping review; the source for the availability / security / ease-of-use
   criteria the interface design was checked against.

3. **Designing and Producing a Telerehabilitation Mobile Application and a
   Web-Based Smart Dashboard for Online Monitoring of Patients at Risk of Stroke
   During COVID-19 Pandemic and Post-Pandemic Era**
   M. Rezapour, N. Nakhostin Ansari.
   *International Journal of Basic Science in Medicine*, 2021.
   [doi:10.34172/ijbsm.2021.xx](https://doi.org/10.34172/ijbsm.2021.xx)
   — Closest precedent: a patient-facing app paired with a clinician dashboard,
   and the model for splitting this project the same way.

4. **Medical Information System As Supporting Tele-Rehabilitation of Post-Stroke
   Patients Using Website-Based Applications**
   S. M. Wijaya, A. Arifin, A. F. Babgei.
   *2022 International Conference*, IEEE.
   — Informed the patient-record and session-history data model.

5. **An Interactive Web Portal for Customised Telerehabilitation in Neurological
   Care**
   M. A. Hannan Bin Azhar, Z. Mészáros, T. Islam, S. K. Manna.
   *2023 IEEE 22nd International Conference*, IEEE.
   — Source for per-patient exercise parameterisation, which became the
   `gain` / hold-duration / repetition settings.

6. **PadayonTa: A Physical Therapy Telerehabilitation Application For Stroke
   Patients**
   A. Fernandez et al. Preprint, May 2022.
   [doi:10.13140/RG.2.2.16168.24323](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.16168.24323)
   — A comparable student-scale system; useful as a scope benchmark.

## Sensing, monitoring and remote examination

7. **Design of a Multi-Sensors Wearable Platform for Remote Monitoring of Knee
   Rehabilitation**
   S. Tedesco, O. Manzano Torre, M. Belcastro, P. Torchia, D. Alfieri,
   L. Khokhlova, S. Dimitrov, et al.
   *IEEE Access*, vol. 10, 2022. Open access.
   — The wearable-sensor alternative to camera-based tracking; the trade-off
   discussion behind choosing a webcam is drawn from here.

8. **A Dual Doctor-Patient Twin Paradigm for Transparent Remote Examination,
   Diagnosis, and Rehabilitation**
   M. Tröbinger, A. Costinescu, H. Xing, J. Elsner, T. Hu, A. Naceri,
   L. Figueredo, E. Jensen, D. Wollherr, S. Haddadin.
   *2021 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems
   (IROS)*, IEEE.
   — Motivates keeping the clinician in the loop rather than fully automating
   assessment.

9. **Tele-Treatment Application Design for Disabled Patients with Wireless
   Sensors**
   A. Arteta Albert, L. F. de Mingo López, N. Gómez Blas.
   *Applied Sciences*, 10(3):1142, February 2020. Open access.
   [doi:10.3390/app10031142](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10031142)
   — Application-architecture reference for sensor-driven remote treatment.

## Adoption and acceptance

10. **Perception of Telemedicine Among Orthopedic Patients**
    *Indian Journal of Public Health*, 2023. Published by Wolters Kluwer –
    Medknow.
    — Patient-acceptance evidence; the argument for a low-friction interface
    with no wearable hardware.
